# Ken Awase (Saito / Kobayashi)

Uchitachi (打太刀 - Sword Attacker) initiates movements.  Instead of just receiving attacks (Uke) in grappling, this role is to provide a well placed guided attach to help teach the partner.

Shitachi/uketachi (仕太刀 - Sword performer/receiver) performs technique. 

1. Both partners raise for tsugi-ashi shomenuchi.  Shitachi moves offline hiraki-ashi to Uchitachi's wrist.
   - Alternate: Target head
2. Uchitachi prepares a deep tsugi-ashi tsuki thrust, pushing shitachi's sword offline if shitachi doesn't move.  Shitachi steps tenshin, raising sword.  Uchitachi returns to original chudan-no-kamae.  Shitachi steps around and does large yokomen to wrist.
   - Alternate: Uchitachi doesn't leave thrust position.  Shitachi cuts wrist. (no foot movement, just hip shift to be aligned forward)
3. Start as (1). Shitachi lets Uchitachi raise sword (or uchitachi responds quickly) stepping back, but matches movement sliding in with Tsuki to neck.
4.  Both raise, meet in cut together.   Uchitachi pushes Shitachi's sword, but shitachi slips around and does Tsuki to neck while ayumi-ashi to outside.
5. Uchitachi prepares tsugi-ashi shomenuchi.  Shitachi does ukenagashi, steps around in yokomen to outside wrist.  Uchitachi raises (either quickly or after Shitachi catches and releases wrist), stepping back.  Shitachi enters ayumi-ashi, cutting outside wrist from below.
   - Alternate: block both wrists out.
6. Perform (3), then do ukenagashi, completing as (5).
7. Uchitachi starts raise for shomenuchi.  Shitachi enters tsugi-ashi, blocking out both wrists.  Then ukenagashi, hiraki-ashi yokomenuchi to neck.

Footwork:

- Suri-ashi (Sliding Feet): The foundation of all Japanese arts, where feet slide across the floor without lifting to maintain constant contact, reducing friction and enabling fast movement.
  - Slide: Okuri-ashi (Trailing Foot): The standard, most used movement. Move the right foot forward (or backward), then quickly bring the left foot up to match, maintaining the initial distance between feet.
  - Tsugi-ashi (Subsequent Foot): A, step used to close distance. The left foot moves up to touch or pass the right foot before the right foot moves again to strike.
  - Hiraki-ashi (Diagonal/Open Foot): A movement to evade or create angles, where you move diagonally to the right or left, often with a slight pivot.
    - From right hanmi moving right: the right foot will move first in a Okuri-ashi manner, but the left foot pivots out (just like the end of a yokomen strike).
    - From right hanmi moving left: the left foot moves first, forward and out to become the front foot, then the right foot follows, pivoting (like the end of yokomen).
  - Step: Ayumi-ashi (Walking Feet): A natural walking movement used for traversing longer distances quickly, rarely used in close combat.
