# Additional Foundations

## Footwork

###  Suri-ashi (Sliding Feet)
The foundation of all Japanese arts, where feet slide across the floor without lifting to maintain constant contact, reducing friction and enabling fast movement.

  - Okuri-ashi (Trailing Foot / Slide): Move the right foot forward (or backward), then quickly bring the left foot up to match, maintaining the initial distance between feet.

  - Ayumi-ashi (Walking Feet / Step): A natural walking movement used for traversing longer distances quickly, rarely used in close combat.

  - Tsugi-ashi (Subsequent Foot / Push): A step used to close distance. The left foot moves up to touch or pass the right foot before the right foot moves again to strike.

  - Hiraki-ashi (Open Foot / Switch/Pivot): A movement to evade or create angles, where you move diagonally to the right or left, often with a slight pivot.
    - From right hanmi moving right: the right foot will move first in an Okuri-ashi manner, but the left foot pivots out (just like the end of a yokomen-uchi strike).
    - From right hanmi moving left: the left foot moves first, forward and out to become the front foot, then the right foot follows, pivoting (like the end of yokomen-uchi).

## Ken/Tachi Foundations

### Base Poses

#### Chūdan-no-Kamae 中段の構え (Middle Stance)

- Chūdan - Middle level
- Seigan 正眼: holding one's sword pointing towards the opponent's eyes (Seigan-no-kamae is an older reference to this stance)

#### Jōdan-no-Kamae 上段の構え (High Stance)

- Jōdan - Top level
- sword raised above head, hilt aligned directly above the front of the face, sword pointing backwards up at 45 degrees

### Base Waza

- [Kanai video goes through the foundational Waza, then starts Kumitachi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qpx6dIaLhlc)

#### Suriage Men 摺上げ面
- Sliding parry.  Parry on the upswing while sliding in.
- use hips, tsuka slightly out, not taking kissaki offline

#### Kaeshi Suriage Men
- sliding parry reverse.  Parry the parry.  Shitachi barely moves, uchitachi is sliding in.

#### Kiriotoshi Tsuki
- strike their sword from kiriotoshi, then tsuki
- hip turns as strike hits.
- can be done stepping back raise/cut as uchitachi does a shōmen-uchi stepping in
   - Response: ukenagashi, ayumi ashi, yokomen-uchi/shōmen-uchi to head

#### Suriage Kote
- like suriage men, but sidestepping ayumi ashi as we move in.
- sword travels up, ends up loaded on right shoulder from the sidestep
- readjust above head, strike kote

#### Makiotoshi Men
- sword touch, use connection to flip down/out/completely across into all the way behind and shōmen cut
- flip is tip rotational
